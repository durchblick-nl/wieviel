## Zahn-Studie: Anfrage über den Korrespondenzkontakt des Verlags

Quelle: <https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwag109>.
Die Korrespondenzadresse wurde nicht unabhängig verifiziert; keine Adresse erraten.

Subject: Methods and model details request — DOI 10.1093/aje/kwag109

Dear Dr Zhou and colleagues,

We are evaluating whether tooth loss can add useful information to an experimental
Swiss life-expectancy calculator. We have read the public abstract and the public
supplement, including the added-sugar sensitivity analysis in Table S1.

Would you be willing to share an author manuscript and clarify:

- The exact primary-model tooth-loss estimates, their uncertainty and covariates.
- How tooth loss was ascertained, including third molars, congenital absence,
  orthodontic extractions and implants.
- The absolute survival/life-table method, age range, follow-up horizon, baseline
  hazards and assumptions used to estimate life-expectancy differences.
- Whether reproducible code and a public NHANES variable/cycle specification are
  available, and the terms for using the model in a public calculator.
- Whether any one-leg-stance measure was available in the source data; we understand
  that NHANES modified Romberg testing is not the CLINIMEX 10-second test.

We would not treat associations as causal treatment effects or combine unrelated
published risk ratios without further modelling. Thank you for your consideration.

## Einbeinstand: CLINIMEX

Quelle: <https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2021-105360>.
Verifizierter Korrespondenzkontakt im Originalartikel: cgaraujo@iis.com.br.

Subject: Data/model clarification request — 10-second one-legged stance and survival

Dear Dr Araújo,

We are assessing your 2022 10-second one-legged stance study as a possible predictor
for an experimental Swiss life-expectancy model.

Could you clarify the discrepancy between Table 2 model 3 (HR 1.84, 95% CI
1.22–2.77, p=0.003) and the abstract (1.23–2.78, p<0.001)? We would also appreciate
information on available deidentified data or model code, baseline survival, age
effects, exposure prevalence, and any repeat measurements or external validation.

For a joint model, were tooth status, smoking, physical activity, diet or relevant
medication available in later data collections? If data can be shared on reasonable
request as stated in the article, what application, ethical review, collaboration
and usage terms would be required? No patient-level records should be emailed to us.

We would not interpret improved test performance as a demonstrated life-extension
effect. Thank you for your consideration.

## Gemeinsame Daten: ELSA-Verfügbarkeit vor Zugangsbeantragung bestätigen

Offizieller Kontakt der Zugangsseite: elsa@ucl.ac.uk.
Quelle: <https://www.elsa-project.ac.uk/accessing-elsa-data>.

Subject: Feasibility of joint oral-health, one-leg balance and mortality analysis

Dear ELSA data team,

We are investigating the feasibility of a joint survival model including dental
status and measured one-leg standing performance, alongside lifestyle factors.
The nurse guide describes an eyes-open 30-second leg-rise test for eligible
participants aged 69 and under. We understand that this is not automatically
interchangeable with the CLINIMEX test and that linked mortality data have
additional access restrictions.

Could you confirm which waves contain natural-tooth count or an edentulism measure,
the exact timed one-leg variables and protocols, and compatible mortality/censoring
data? Are these available together for a licensed research analysis, through which
access route, and under what restrictions on commercial/public prediction tools?

We are seeking feasibility guidance, not requesting individual records by email.
Thank you for your help.
